01

The question

Standard models treat index returns as a continuous, linear, roughly random process — the textbook Efficient Market Hypothesis. But Indian equity markets are punctuated by sharp, institution-driven regime shifts: FII outflows during global risk-off episodes, DII buying during domestic SIP-fuelled rallies. A discrete-time Markov chain captures something a linear regression can't — the probability that today's regime determines tomorrow's, and how that probability itself shifts depending on who's moving money.

02

Building the state space

Every trading day is classified as Upward, Downward, or Stagnant based on whether its log return crosses a symmetric threshold. Too tight, and noise gets mistaken for a regime shift. Too wide, and real momentum gets buried in "Stagnant." Drag the slider through the same 0.20–0.40% range tested in the dissertation — this is live on a real, independent Nifty 50 sample that grows every trading day.

03

Reading institutional flow

Fixed rupee-value cutoffs go stale as market size grows over a multi-year window. Instead, each day's net FII/DII flow is ranked against its own empirical distribution — anything above the 75th percentile is Strongly Positive (SP), below the 25th is Strongly Negative (SN), the rest is Neutral (N).

06

What the model found

These are the dissertation's actual empirical results on the original dataset — 1,612 trading days of Nifty 50 closes with matched FII/DII net flows, April 2018 to October 2024 — plus an independent replication that keeps growing with fresh data every trading day.

41.29%
Upward-state diagonal dominance (p₁₁), with a Mean First Passage Time of just 2.56 days back to Upward — evidence against the strong-form Efficient Market Hypothesis.
41.31%
Downward persistence when FII flow is Strongly Negative — sustained foreign selling measurably prolongs bear regimes.
1.70→1.28d
Expected bear-market duration shortens from 1.703 to 1.276 days once DII buying (SP regime) takes over.
23.06%
The "DII shock absorber": under sustained Domestic Institutional buying, the market's long-run steady-state probability of sitting in a bear regime collapses to 23.06% — evidence that SIP-driven domestic liquidity has structurally decoupled Indian equities from pure foreign-flow dependence. (One methodology, one market, one time window — not a settled claim.)
36.1% vs 15.6%
Independent replication. Re-running this exact methodology on a fresh, non-overlapping sample reproduces the same shape of result: Downward persistence is markedly higher under sustained institutional selling than under sustained buying. See the live model above — Section 05's "SN" and "SP" driving-matrix options are this exact replication data, updated automatically.
